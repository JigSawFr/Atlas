# Atlas's website

`https://atlas.boreale.dev/` (until its domain is set: `https://jigsawfr.github.io/Atlas/`; every link inside it is relative, so it works at both): the project page and the explorer, in the fifteen languages of Boréale's sites.

```sh
git submodule update --init               # library/boreale-site, what Boréale's sites share
git clone --depth 1 -b data https://github.com/JigSawFr/Atlas data   # the data branch
python3 tools/build_site.py build data    # the pages, data/ and x/ (some 80 MB)
python3 tools/check_site.py build         # links, catalogs, CSP, the explorer's shards, its size (Pages: 1 GB)
python3 -m http.server -d build           # then http://localhost:8000/
SITE_AXE=axe.min.js python3 library/boreale-site/tools/site_layout.py build --skip data --skip x   # phones to desktop, with axe (Playwright; site.yml's adds the explorer's views)
```

- `template.html` is every page's frame; `hero.html` the top of the project page (its title beside a drawing of what the files hold: one made-up game tied across four stores by `links.json`, and this week's trending games, in HTML and inline SVG with the catalog's labels), `home.html` the rest of it, `explore.html` the explorer (`assets/explore.js`), `status.html` the data's status (`status_body()` in `build_site.py`, from the data branch's `report.json` and `index.json`: each source's and platform's last day, marked late past its rhythm, 2 days or 9, against the report's day, and by `assets/site.js` against today; a note when there is no report). Each `{{key}}` is a text of `i18n/<language>.json`; the explorer's texts are the catalog's `x_` keys, put in the page as JSON data.
- `build_site.py` publishes the data branch as it is under `data/`, and `site_data.py` cuts it for the explorer under `x/<platform>/`: `lists.json` (the best known, the trending, the coming soon), `names.json` (for the search), `facets.json` (each facet, genre, mode, series, developer, publisher, theme, perspective, tag and year, with its values and their game counts: the filters' menus, some 26 kB), `browse/<page>.json` (every named game, best known first, 5 000 a page of some 380 kB, with the rows of the page that have each facet value: the explorer fetches the next page only when it needs more) `u/<shard>.json` with `u/meta.json` (one sheet for each game on several of the platforms the data has, by its Wikidata item: the platforms' figures side by side, the traits' union, the games alike and the other stores; a platform's own sheet says `unified` and the explorer goes to it), and `g/<shard>.json` (each game's sheet: what every file says of it, its ids on the other stores). A sheet costs one small file; the explorer reads nothing else from anywhere.
- **The explorer's views** (`assets/explore.js`, its address says what is shown): the lists (`#<platform>/popular`, `trending`, `upcoming`), the search, a game's sheet (`#<platform>/game/<id>`, or `#game/<Wikidata item>` for a game on several platforms; `?alone` asks for one platform's) and **Browse** (`#<platform>/browse?genres=…&tags=…&sort=critics`): the facet menus come from `facets.json`, the games from `browse/<page>.json`, a page at a time (the first one, then the next when the list wants more, 50 games at a time). Every value picked narrows the list (a game must have all of them) and the counts follow the loaded pages (shown as `≥ n` while some are not in yet). The sorts other than the best known cover what is loaded, until "Load every game". The facets' values are Wikidata's and the stores' words, not translated.
- **One name for one thing** (`tools/taxonomy.py`): each source has its own words for a game's kind (Wikidata's "role-playing video game", the Microsoft Store's "Role-playing (RPG)", Steam's tag "RPG"). `site_data.py` ties them to one *notion* (114 of them, with the words the sources use for each; 564 words), whatever the kind the source filed it under (Steam's tag "Singleplayer" is a mode, IGDB's theme "Action" a genre, Steam's tag "Pixel Graphics" a visual style): *genres* (with their parents: a platformer is also an action game in the filters, though not on the sheet), *themes*, *modes*, *perspectives*, and what the players' tags say, in *styles* (visual), *moods*, *gameplay*, *content* and *access* (indie, free to play, early access). A word followed by "video game" or "game" is the word without it. A notion has its name in every language (`x_<letter>_<key>`: g genres, t themes, m modes, p perspectives, y styles, o moods, h gameplay, c content, a access) and its icon (Lucide's, `site/icons/`, drawn from a sprite the explorer's page holds: `i-<letter>-<key>`, and `i-k-<kind>` for the kinds of facets). What no notion names stays as the sources wrote it, tidied (a capital, no "video game"); studios and publishers are one by their name without its legal words ("Nintendo Co., Ltd.", "NINTENDO" and "Nintendo"), series and tags that differ only by case or signs are one, all in the spelling most games use. Names lose the trademark signs and the stray spaces. To add a notion: a line in `CONCEPTS` with its icon (copy it from Lucide into `site/icons/`) and its 15 names in the catalogs; `tests/test_taxonomy.py` checks all three.
- **Technical details** (`tools/tech.py`, from the stores' own sources in `sources/`, which `site_data.py` reads: `steam-store/apps`, `gog/catalog`, `msstore/products`): where a game runs, the Steam Deck's verdict, the most players (Microsoft), what it offers (achievements, cloud saves, controller support, cross-play, Play Anywhere, 4K, HDR, 60 fps…), its languages and voices (Steam's language numbers as BCP 47 codes; the page writes their names with `Intl.DisplayNames`), its age ratings (PEGI, ESRB, USK, CERO) and the *names* of its pictures (`img`: `h` horizontal, `v` vertical, `t` thumbnail, `s` screenshots), which the page does not draw yet. Each store's words become one set (Steam's category numbers, GOG's slugs, Microsoft's capability names), and a key is left out when the store says nothing. The sheet of a game on several platforms keeps each one's.
- **What Wikidata says** (`tools/wdfacts.py`, from `sources/wikidata/facts-*.json`): a game's official site, age ratings (the stores' first, Wikidata's for the systems they do not give), director, composer and screenwriter (three at most each), engine, PCGamingWiki page and trailers on YouTube (three at most). The sheet draws them as *Credits*, *Engine* and *Links*; the links are outgoing (`noreferrer`), no player is embedded, so the visitor does not contact YouTube until they click. One query for each property or group, so a failed one costs only its own file; the facts are the game's, the same on each platform (`wd`, once on a sheet of all its platforms).
- The look is Boréale's (`branding/README.md` in JigSawFr/boreale), and so are the icons, the favicons and the preview image in `assets/` (`branding/atlas/`, copied by its `tools/sync_branding.py`: change them there): the night, the aurora once at the top, Sora, Figtree and JetBrains Mono served with the site. What Boréale's sites share is [boreale-site](https://github.com/JigSawFr/boreale-site), the submodule `library/boreale-site`: the fonts, the marks (`marks/atlas.svg` is Atlas's: the frame in the aurora's colours, a folded map and the pole star in snow), `boreale.css` and `boreale.js` (the colours, the language menu and the menu of Boréale's sites), `chrome.css` and `chrome.js` (the page chrome: sky and aurora, header, sections, buttons, cards, band, footer), `lang.js`, `umami.js` and the texts of the two menus, copied into `assets/` at the build (`boreale.copy_assets()`); `social-preview.html` is the source of `assets/social-preview.png`, taken with Chromium at 1280 × 640.
- **The header**, as PlayGuard's: the home page's sections and the status as plain links, **Explore** lit by the aurora to the explorer, GitHub, then the languages; on a phone the links on a second row to scroll, Explore first. The header, the footer and the sky come from boreale-site (`Site.header()`, `Site.footer()`, `Site.sky()`, written by `build_site.py`). The home page has the aurora drawn by `chrome.js` behind its header and hero, the other pages a quieter sky. The name opens the menu of Boréale's sites (boreale-site's `Site.switcher()` and `sites.json`), a popover placed by its `boreale.js`. A link to another of them keeps the origin and carries `utm_source=atlas&utm_medium=nav` (`Site.links_out()`, over every page). Unlike Lantern's, it shows no versions: the build reads nothing from boreale.dev.
- The Content-Security-Policy allows nothing but the site itself: no inline script (the explorer's texts are `application/json` data), no style attribute, no font or script from elsewhere; the one address a page may send to is Umami's (`connect-src`).
- **The audience**, counted by Umami Cloud (<https://cloud.umami.is>): `assets/umami.js` (boreale-site's copy) is its tracker, served with the site, that sends each page seen to `gateway.umami.is`, without cookie, and only from the site's own address (`SITE_URL`'s, not a local copy). Its site in Umami (boreale-site's `UMAMI_WEBSITE`) is Boréale's, shared with the other projects' sites: Umami's *Hostname* filter tells them apart. The footer links to boreale.dev's privacy and legal notice, which says what it counts.
- `.github/workflows/site.yml` builds it and publishes it to GitHub Pages after each successful `build` or `daily` run, at each push to main touching it, and on demand.
