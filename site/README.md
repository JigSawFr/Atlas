# Atlas's website

`https://atlas.boreale.dev/` (until its domain is set: `https://jigsawfr.github.io/Atlas/`; every link inside it is relative, so it works at both): the project page and the explorer, in the fifteen languages of Boréale's sites.

```sh
git submodule update --init               # library/boreale-site, what Boréale's sites share
git clone --depth 1 -b data https://github.com/JigSawFr/Atlas data   # the data branch
python3 tools/build_site.py build data    # the pages, data/ and x/ (some 80 MB)
python3 tools/check_site.py build         # links, catalogs, CSP, the explorer's shards, its size (Pages: 1 GB)
python3 -m http.server -d build           # then http://localhost:8000/
SITE_AXE=axe.min.js python3 library/boreale-site/tools/site_layout.py build --skip data --skip x   # phones to desktop, with axe (Playwright; site.yml's adds the explorer's views)
```

- `template.html` is every page's frame; `hero.html` the top of the project page (its title beside a drawing of what the files hold: one made-up game tied across four stores by `links.json`, and this week's trending games, in HTML and inline SVG with the catalog's labels), `home.html` the rest of it, `explore.html` the explorer (`assets/explore.js`), `status.html` the data's status (`status_body()` in `build_site.py`, from the data branch's `report.json` and `index.json`: each source's and platform's last day, marked late past its rhythm, 2 days or 9, against the report's day, and by `assets/site.js` against today; a note when there is no report). Each `{{key}}` is a text of `i18n/<language>.json`; the explorer's texts are the catalog's `x_` keys, put in the page as JSON data.
- `build_site.py` publishes the data branch as it is under `data/`, and `site_data.py` cuts it for the explorer under `x/<platform>/`: `lists.json` (the best known, the trending, the coming soon), `names.json` (for the search), `facets.json` (each facet, genre, mode, series, developer, publisher, theme, perspective, tag and year, with its values and their game counts: the filters' menus, some 26 kB), `browse/<page>.json` (every named game, best known first, 5 000 a page of some 380 kB, with the rows of the page that have each facet value: the explorer fetches the next page only when it needs more) `u/<shard>.json` with `u/meta.json` (one sheet for each game on several of the platforms the data has, by its Wikidata item: the platforms' figures side by side, the traits' union, the games alike and the other stores; a platform's own sheet says `unified` and the explorer goes to it), and `g/<shard>.json` (each game's sheet: what every file says of it, its ids on the other stores). A sheet costs one small file; the explorer reads nothing else from anywhere.
- **The explorer's views** (`assets/explore.js`, its address says what is shown): the lists (`#<platform>/popular`, `trending`, `upcoming`), the search, a game's sheet (`#<platform>/game/<id>`, or `#game/<Wikidata item>` for a game on several platforms; `?alone` asks for one platform's) and **Browse** (`#<platform>/browse?genres=…&tags=…&sort=critics`): the facet sections come from `facets.json`, the games from `browse/<page>.json`, a page at a time (the first one, then the next when the list wants more, 50 games at a time). Every value picked narrows the list (a game must have all of them) and the counts follow the loaded pages (shown as `≥ n` while some are not in yet). The sorts other than the best known cover what is loaded, until "Load every game". The facets' values are Wikidata's and the stores' words, not translated.
- **One name for one thing** (`tools/taxonomy.py`): each source has its own words for a game's kind (Wikidata's "role-playing video game", the Microsoft Store's "Role-playing (RPG)", Steam's tag "RPG"). `site_data.py` ties them to one *notion* (114 of them, with the words the sources use for each; 564 words), whatever the kind the source filed it under (Steam's tag "Singleplayer" is a mode, IGDB's theme "Action" a genre, Steam's tag "Pixel Graphics" a visual style): *genres* (with their parents: a platformer is also an action game in the filters, though not on the sheet), *themes*, *modes*, *perspectives*, and what the players' tags say, in *styles* (visual), *moods*, *gameplay*, *content* and *access* (indie, free to play, early access). A word followed by "video game" or "game" is the word without it. A notion has its name in every language (`x_<letter>_<key>`: g genres, t themes, m modes, p perspectives, y styles, o moods, h gameplay, c content, a access) and its icon (Lucide's, `site/icons/`, drawn from a sprite the explorer's page holds: `i-<letter>-<key>`, and `i-k-<kind>` for the kinds of facets). What no notion names stays as the sources wrote it, tidied (a capital, no "video game"); studios and publishers are one by their name without its legal words ("Nintendo Co., Ltd.", "NINTENDO" and "Nintendo"), series and tags that differ only by case or signs are one, all in the spelling most games use. Names lose the trademark signs and the stray spaces. To add a notion: a line in `CONCEPTS` with its icon (copy it from Lucide into `site/icons/`) and its 15 names in the catalogs; `tests/test_taxonomy.py` checks all three.
- **Technical details** (`tools/tech.py`, from the stores' own sources in `sources/`, which `site_data.py` reads: `steam-store/apps`, `gog/catalog`, `msstore/products`): where a game runs, the Steam Deck's verdict, the most players (Microsoft), what it offers (achievements, cloud saves, controller support, cross-play, Play Anywhere, 4K, HDR, 60 fps…), its languages and voices (Steam's language numbers as BCP 47 codes; the page writes their names with `Intl.DisplayNames`), its age ratings (PEGI, ESRB, USK, CERO) and the *names* of its pictures (`img`: `h` horizontal, `v` vertical, `t` thumbnail, `s` screenshots), which the page does not draw yet. Each store's words become one set (Steam's category numbers, GOG's slugs, Microsoft's capability names), and a key is left out when the store says nothing. The sheet of a game on several platforms keeps each one's.
- **What Wikidata says** (`tools/wdfacts.py`, from `sources/wikidata/facts-*.json`): a game's official site, age ratings (the stores' first, Wikidata's for the systems they do not give), director, composer and screenwriter (three at most each), engine, PCGamingWiki page and trailers on YouTube (three at most). The sheet draws them as *Credits*, *Engine* and *Links*; the links are outgoing (`noreferrer`), no player is embedded, so the visitor does not contact YouTube until they click. One query for each property or group, so a failed one costs only its own file; the facts are the game's, the same on each platform (`wd`, once on a sheet of all its platforms).
- **Cards** (`explore.js` `tile()`, `card()`; `site.css`): a sheet's alike games and editions are cards with a picture; a button at the end of the views (`#x-layout`, kept in the browser under `atlas-cards`) turns the lists, the browse view and the search into cards too. A tile's picture is Steam's `header.jpg` by the app id (through `wsrv.nl`) for Steam games; the other platforms' rows end with the name of one (`site_data.py` `picture()`: `s<app>` Steam's header of the same game, by `links.json` or the game's own Steam app, `i<id>` IGDB's cover, its top kept, and in the three lists only `g<name>` GOG's own cover, whose names would make a page of the browse index half again as heavy; the sheets' alike games carry the short ones too); else a cover drawn by the page. **No sheet is fetched for a tile** (a shard of sheets is some 160 kB and a page of 50 games would touch dozens). Measured on the 2026-11-07 data: the browse pages 12 % heavier for the Switch, Xbox and PlayStation 4, GOG's `lists.json` 16 to 41 kB; 161 of the Switch's 200 best known games have a picture, 175 of Xbox's, all of GOG's. Texts `x_as_cards`, `x_as_list`: written by the session, not proofread.
- **A sheet's tiles and rings** (`explore.js` `platTile()`, `ring()`; `site.css`): the head's rings are the renown, the critics, the players' rating (IGDB, from 20 ratings) and each store's share of good reviews (their counts stay in the figures under them); help texts `x_players_help`, `x_store_help`. A platform's tile under "By platform" has its own colour (`TINTS`: the colour its machines and stores are known by, a colour and not a mark; a hue from its id for the others) and, large and faint behind it, its short name with a computer or a console drawn beside (`i-d-pc` Lucide's `monitor`, `i-d-console` its `gamepad-2`): **no brand's logo**, Nintendo and Microsoft had theirs taken out of Simple Icons, and the platforms' owners keep their marks. The cards of a sheet and of the figures (alike games, editions, best rated, gems) are one size each (3:2, 4:3 on a phone, two a row), the picture filling the card under a shade, the name (two lines at most) and the figures laid over it.
- **Tiles in detail** (`explore.js` `platformTiles()`, `card()`, `sharedLine()`; `tools/site_data.py` `shared_traits()`; `site.css`): a platform's tile has a small ring for its renown (`miniRing()`), its release with how long after the first it came ("+10 months", `gap()`, in the visitor's language by `Intl.NumberFormat` units; "First" on the earliest when the dates differ), a star on the one the game is best known on, Game Pass and Steam Deck (verified or playable) chips, and a link to its store (`↗`, Steam and Xbox: their ids are the stores'; GOG's are numbers and PlayStation's Wikidata items, no page to name). A card of an alike game says **how alike** (`x_alike_pct`: the figure was always the similarity, not the renown the tooltip said) and shows up to three notions the two games have in common, the rarest first (the build adds them to each alike row as `"<letter>:<key>"`, `[id, name, similarity, picture, shared]`, the empty tail left out; sheets are 8 to 13 % heavier: Switch 7.0 to 7.6 MB, Steam 69 to 77 MB, `u/` 23 to 26 MB), and, on a sheet of several platforms, the platform of a game that is on one only. On a phone the long rows of cards (alike games, best rated, gems) scroll sideways. On a fine pointer that hovers, a card and a tile lean toward the pointer and catch a glint (custom properties `--rx --ry --mx --my` set by script, none with reduced motion). New texts: `x_best_platform`, `x_later`, `x_first`, `x_alike_pct`, `x_open_store`, `x_in_common`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread.
- **Moving about a sheet** (`explore.js` `sectionBar()`, `distribution()`; `site.css`): once the head has left the window a bar slides in with the game, its score and one button per section (the one in view marked, `aria-current`; buttons and not links, the address belongs to the router; inert while hidden; no animation with reduced motion). Each notion's chip says how many games of the platform have it (`x/<platform>/facets.json`, the browse view's own, asked after the sheet is drawn; `x_trait_count` as its tooltip). Under the rank chips, a small histogram puts the score among the platform's: the 101 cut points of `stats.json`'s `renown.q` in bands of 5, one line for the game, linear because the skew (most games are little known) is the point; the sentence under it is its text. New texts: `x_sections`, `x_trait_count`, `x_dist_title`, `x_dist_help`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread.
- **More on a sheet** (`explore.js` `timeline()`, `pager()`, `heart()`, `favoritesView()`, `compareView()`; `tools/site_data.py` `families()`): a game on several platforms with different release dates gets one axis (a row a platform, a dot where its date falls, the date as text). A row clicked in a list remembers the list in `sessionStorage` (`atlas-trail`: its address, scroll, how many rows, its games in order, 400 at most): the sheet shows `‹ n / N ›` and the left and right arrows step through it, and the back link brings the list back scrolled and as long as it was. A heart on a sheet keeps the game in `localStorage` (`atlas-favs`, 60 at most, `<platform>/<id>` or `u/<Wikidata item>` and a name; this browser only, nothing sent); `#<platform>/favorites` lists them, two or three ticked open `#<platform>/compare?g=…`, a table, a game a column, the highest of each figure starred. The build adds `family` to the sheet of a game whose first series has other named games (`[series, [[id, name, release, picture], …]]`, 12 at most, the game itself kept, none for a "series" of more than 30 games: on Steam those are publishers' and stores' labels); it weighs about 7 MB over the site (253 to 260 MB). The sections' entrance is the class `unveil`: `reveal` is the shared base's, which hides every `.card` under it until its own script adds `in`. New texts: `x_timeline`, `x_prev`, `x_next`, `x_favorites`, `x_fav_add`, `x_fav_remove`, `x_fav_empty`, `x_fav_note`, `x_compare`, `x_compare_n`, `x_compare_pick`, `x_highest`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread. The way back to the list (`.back.fab`, bottom left), the steps through it (`.pager.fab`, a pill at the bottom, centred) and the heart (`.fav.fab`, bottom right) float over the sheet, outside the head (its entrance animation would be their frame); the head's line keeps only the chip back to the overall sheet and the store button, and is not there when neither exists. Under the title, a sheet of several platforms has no platform badges (the tiles are those) and no line at all; one platform keeps its badge and id. The Wikidata item is among the links ("Wikidata · Q…"), not in the head.
- **The game's sheet and a platform's** (`explore.js` `renderSheet()` `multi`, `atlasScore()`, `recipe()`): a game on several platforms has a sheet of the whole (`#game/<Wikidata item>`) that holds what they share (pictures, traits, text, team, links, the tiles and the release axis) and what is worked out across them: an **Atlas score**, the stores' reviews pooled, how many platforms, the renown on average. What belongs to one platform lives on that platform's own sheet (`#<platform>/game/<id>?alone`, reached from its tile, with a chip back to the whole): its store's reviews and figures, its rank among its games and where the score falls, the counts on notions, the technical details, Game Pass and Steam Deck, a link to its store; the sheet takes the platform's colour and a watermark, as its tile does. Atlas score = the mean of four signals, each 0 to 100, weighted then renormalised over those that exist (at least one of the first three is needed): critics 35 % (mean of the platforms' scores); store reviews 30 % x n / (n + 100) (positive shares pooled by review count, pulled toward 70 as if 40 reviews at 70 had been added); players 15 % x n / (n + 30) (IGDB's rating from 20 ratings, 30 at 70 added); reach 20 % (square root of the best renown, back on 100). The sheet shows the table. It is worked out in the browser from the sheet's own figures, so a list cannot sort on it yet. New texts: `x_atlas_score`, `x_atlas_help`, `x_atlas_how`, `x_atlas_note`, `x_sig_store`, `x_sig_reach`, `x_signal`, `x_value`, `x_weight`, `x_pooled_label`, `x_pooled_value`, `x_score_avg`, `x_global_sheet`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread.
- **Editions** (`tools/editions.py` `by_name()`, `tools/site_data.py` `edition_map()`, `alike()`): a game's other editions ("X - Gold Edition", "X Deluxe", a season pass) are one game with it. titledb's mapping (the Switch) and, on every platform, a name that is another's plus edition words from a company they share (a developer or a publisher) give `edition -> game`. The game is the one with no edition word in its name; a plain name is never an edition (two "Resident Evil 4" are two games) and an edition with no such game stays apart. Editions are out of the lists, the browse view, the figures, the alike lists (an edition becomes its game, the game itself and its own editions are dropped, once each) and the series strip; the game's sheet lists them (`editions`), an edition's sheet says it is one (`edition_of`) and is not part of the sheet of all platforms. The search finds an edition by its name and opens its game (`names.json` rows point at the game). Steam is the least covered (25 here: its editions often come from another publisher); real DLC is not in the data.
- **Figures** (`#<platform>/stats`; `site_data.py` `stats()`, `explore.js` `statsView()`; `site.css`): condensed at the build into `x/<platform>/stats.json` (some 3 kB) and `x/stats.json` (every platform's headline figures, 1.5 kB), so the page reads two small files: the named games, the critics' average and median and their spread in tens, the share with a critics' score, the renown's average and its 101 quantiles, the releases of the last 30 years with their critics' average, the 12 most common genres, studios and publishers (3 rated games at least) with theirs, the modes, the best rated (the best known quarter's, by renown: a score from a handful of critics is noise among the unknown) and the hidden gems (85 or more, renown between its first quartile and its median). Drawn in one hue, Boréale's ice, one measure a chart: headline numbers that count up, columns with a tooltip and a table under them (`<details>`), horizontal bars that link to the browse view filtered, cards. A sheet says where its game stands on its best known platform (`x_rank`, from the quantiles; `x_vs_above`/`x_vs_below`, against the critics' average), asked after the sheet is drawn. 22 texts (`x_stats`, `x_st_*`, `x_rank`, `x_vs_*`): written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread.
- **Motion** (`site.css`, the end; `explore.js` `bars()`, `countUp()`, `reveal()`): only for visitors who have not asked for less (`prefers-reduced-motion`): the bars grow and the rings fill, a list's first 40 rows rise in one after the other, a sheet's head and figures rise, its sections rise as they are scrolled to (`IntersectionObserver`), the headline figures count up, a sheet's backdrop drifts slowly, a card's picture leans in on hover, the viewer's picture zooms in. With less motion everything is in place at once.
- **Help and folds** (`explore.js` `ring()`; `site.css`): a sheet's rings say what each measures in their tooltip (`title`: `x_score_help`, `x_critics_help`…); nothing opens on a press. Browse's facet menus fold under one "Filters" summary that counts the values picked, open on a wide screen and folded on a phone at first. On a sheet the page's heading folds to its eyebrow and a short title (`x-on-sheet` on `body`), the tabs are hidden (the round arrow goes back), and under 900px the picker and the search scope go too, leaving the search. **The first page** (`explore.js` `homeView()`, `#x-home`; `site.css`, "The first page"): an address without a view (`#steam`, or none) opens rows of cards that scroll sideways (seen recently, trending, coming soon, the best known), each with "See all n" toward its list; `#platform/popular` and the others are as before. Coming soon is grouped by month, each game with how far away it is (`Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`). A sheet has "Compare with…" (`x_compare_with`, to `#platform/favorites?pick=<key>&n=<name>`: the game is ticked on the favorites page, whether it is one or not) and a sheet of several platforms takes a soft colour of its own from the game's id (`.sheet.tinted`). **Seen recently, help, export** (`explore.js` `noteRecent()`, `suggest()`, the `?` dialog, `favoritesView()`): the last 12 sheets opened are kept in this browser (`atlas-recent`) and offered under the search box when it is focused and empty (`x_recent`); `?` opens a dialog of the keys (`x_help_*`); the favorites can be exported as a JSON file (`x_fav_export`; it carries `schema: 1`); the screenshots' places shimmer until the pictures come; from a list to a sheet the picture pressed flies into the sheet's (View Transitions, `view-transition-name: game-pic`, only where the browser has them and motion is not reduced). **Share and cards**: a round button beside the heart (`share()`, `x_share`, `x_link_copied`, the Lucide `share-2` icon) opens the system's sheet where there is one and else copies the address; a row of cards that scrolls sideways fades at its edge on a phone. **The content of a sheet**: the traits fall in two groups (what the game is, then series, studios; a rule between), the awards wear a trophy and fold after four behind "See all n", the interest curve marks its peak and its latest week and reads the week under the pointer (`x_views_peak`, `x_views_ago`), the links are named for what they are ("Wikidata", "Trailer" `x_trailer`) and the release timeline appears only when the dates are more than 14 days apart (the tiles already say "+1 day"). The head has a line under the title (genres, studio, year: `.tagline`), the figures in an even row and the Atlas score's recipe as a quiet link that opens its table. The two help texts are written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread. **More rows and close names** (`explore.js` `homeView()`, `near()`, `favoritesView()`; `tools/site_data.py` `by_release()`): the first page adds "Just out" (`lists.json` `recent`, the games out in the last 60 days, the page showing the last 14), "Your favorites, coming soon" (the favorites found in the coming-soon list) and, asked after the page is up, "On this day" (`x/<platform>/days.json`, the 4 best known games released that day of the year before this year: Steam 69 kB, 28 kB gzipped). A search with no match offers the names a slip away (every word within 1 or 2 letters of a word of the name, `x_near`). The favorites page imports a file saved by Export (`x_fav_import`): a `schema` higher than 1 or no `schema` is refused, keys and platforms it does not know are skipped. New texts: `x_this_day`, `x_out_now`, `x_fav_soon`, `x_near`, `x_fav_import`, `x_fav_imported`, `x_fav_import_bad`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread.
- **The controls** (`site/explore.html`, `explore.js` `buildFacets()`, `browseUi()`, the picker's and the filters' panel code; `site.css`, "The explorer's controls"; `build_site.py` `platform_picker()`): one line holds the **platform picker** (a button with the platform's name and colour; its panel groups the stores and consoles, then the old machines by maker, `MAKERS`, each with its game count and a box to find one, so there is no list of machines apart) and the **search** (`/` focuses it; "All platforms" is the scope, pressed while the search covers every platform, pressed again to go back). The lists are **tabs** under them (Favorites with its count). In Browse the **filters stand in a column** beside the games, sticky from 900px: a facet is a folded section (the first, Genres, is open) with the count of what is picked, its first 6 values and a "See all n" (and a box to find one) when it has more than 8; under 900px the column is a panel rising from the bottom, opened by a "Filters · n" button, closed by its X, the scrim, Escape or "Show n games". Above the games: the count, the values picked as chips (with "Clear filters"), the sort, the cards or rows toggle and a menu (random game, save this view, load every game); what the counts cover is small print under the list (how much was read is no longer shown). A row's figures say what they are: the renown bar, the trend as an arrow, the critics' average as a badge coloured by its value. On a phone (under 700px) a row's picture is its backdrop, blurred under a shade that darkens toward the rank and the name, and the figures fit on one line: the picture's column goes to the text. The cards of a list are all one size (`.results.cards`): one picture ratio, the name on two lines whose room is kept, the figures on two rows pinned to the bottom (renown and trend, then critics and year), each in its own place whatever the game lacks. **The podium**: in a list that ranks (best known, trending, Browse sorted by renown, Atlas score, trend or critics; not by name or year, nor Coming soon, nor a search) `explore.js` `show()` puts `.podium` on the list and `site.css` gives the first three a trophy in gold, silver and bronze (in the rank's place on a row, on the picture's corner on a card) and a tinted outline; no icon is added to the page, the trophy is a mask in the stylesheet. **Comfort**: the search shows up to six **suggestions** under the box (names beginning like the text, then containing it; arrows and Enter, Escape closes; `role="combobox"`); in a list the arrows move from one game to the next (up and down by line, left and right too in cards; up from the first goes back to the search); a list being read shows grey **placeholders** after 150 ms; a search or a Browse that finds nothing shows a box with the next step (clear filters, search every platform, browse with filters); the platform last visited is kept in this browser (`atlas-platform`) and opens an empty address; the podium comes in with a short rise (not when motion is reduced). Under 900px, once the controls are scrolled away, a thin bar (`#x-mini`) keeps the platform, the search and the filters at hand (the first two take the page back to the top). The filters' counts already follow the other filters ticked, and values that would give nothing are greyed. Texts `x_suggestions`, `x_empty_*`. The class names carry no clash with the sheet's (`.topline`, `.side`, `.note` are the sheet's: the controls' are `xc-*`). Texts `x_plat_*`, `x_show_n`, `x_more_options`, `x_see_all`, `x_see_less`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread; `x_machine` and `x_machine_pick` are gone with the select they labelled.
- **Rows, filters, keys** (`explore.js` `row()`, `drawActive()`; `site.css`): a list's row has a small picture (`tile(…, thumb)`: Steam's header, else the drawn cover), its name and its figures side by side from 760px; the values picked in Browse are chips above the list, each one a link to the view without it (text `x_remove_filter`, written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread), and a value the data does not know says "No game found" rather than waiting for pages; `/` goes to the search box and Escape empties it. On a phone (under 700px) the platforms and the lists are one row each, scrolled sideways, the pressed one brought into view.
- **The sheet's body** (`explore.js` `renderSheet()`; `site.css`): from 1000px two columns, the story on the left (screenshots, traits, Wikipedia text, views curve, alike games, editions) and, sticky, the facts on the right (technical details, platforms, team, links, other stores); one column below, same order. On a wide screen the screenshots are the first large and up to four beside it, the last saying how many more there are (`data-more`), a strip on a phone. The facts' column stays in view only when it fits the window (`fitSide()`), else it scrolls with the page. A store's reviews show their share of good ones large, in the rings' colours, and how many there are under it (`x_review_count`, not proofread in the 13 languages other than English and French). The platforms' badges and the "By platform" rows open each platform's own sheet; the alike games leave out the game itself. A screenshot opens in a `<dialog>` (its place among them, "3 / 6", and the next one fetched ahead) (`openViewer()`: arrows, Escape, swipe, the large picture fetched only then; a middle or Ctrl click still opens it in a tab); texts `x_close`, `x_prev`, `x_next`, **written by the session in the 13 languages other than English and French, not proofread**.
- **The head of a sheet** (`explore.js` `backdrop()`, `ring()`; `site.css`): a wide picture fades into the page behind it (Steam's `library_hero.jpg` by its fixed name, else the landscape cover, else the vertical cover blurred; the first that loads, an `error` goes to the next, none leaves the drawn cover), the vertical cover overlaps it, Steam's `logo.png` replaces the visible title when it loads (the `h2` stays for screen readers) and the scores are rings. No new request host (all through `wsrv.nl`), no new text. **Not tried against the real stores**: the fixed names are Steam's convention for games that have them.
- **Pictures** (`explore.js`; the names are `tech.py`'s `img`): a sheet draws a cover (the first store that has a landscape picture: Steam's header, GOG's cover, the Microsoft Store's hero art; else a vertical one: GOG's or Microsoft's poster, else IGDB's cover, which the consoles' games have too: `igdb_cover`, the name of the picture on IGDB's image service, asked with the other IGDB fields and only the name kept; IGDB's first artwork, `igdb_art`, asked with it and drawn at `t_1080p`, is a last backdrop for the head of a sheet whose games no store has a wide picture for. **Neither is tried against the real service until Monday's `build`**: if IGDB refuses the `artworks.image_id` field the batch fails, and the `igdb:` lines of the journal say so), and a strip of up to 6 screenshots (the first store that has some), each linking to a larger one in a new tab (`noreferrer`). **The visitor's browser asks only `wsrv.nl`** (an open source image cache and resize service in the European Union, which asks the store, sends WebP of 8 to 20 kB back and nothing about the visitor on): the Content-Security-Policy's `img-src` is `'self' data: https://wsrv.nl`, the images carry `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` and the screenshots `loading="lazy"`. wsrv.nl sees the visitor's address and the picture's: boreale.dev's privacy notice (`privacy_atlas`) says so, and has to be live before this is. **No picture is copied** into this repository, the `data` branch or the site (the stores' terms do not say we may; the stores' own servers serve them). Where a game has none, or one does not load, the page draws a cover itself (a colour from its name, its first genre's icon): nothing is asked. A store whose pictures are not on the list in `STORE_PICTURES` (and the games neither the stores' sources nor IGDB cover) have the drawn cover. If wsrv.nl goes, the pictures are drawn covers again and `weserv/images` (BSD) can be hosted.
- **Interest** (`views` on a platform's sheet, from `tools/trends.py`'s `curves()`): for the 400 best known Switch and Steam games, a curve of the Wikipedia views a week over the last 13 weeks (the pages of all 8 languages added up, from `sources/wikipedia/daily.json`), drawn as an SVG with a text for screen readers. A game read fewer than 100 times in all, or with less than 4 weeks of views, has none.
- **Awards** (`wd.awards`, from Wikidata's P166 through `tools/wdfacts.py`'s `awards_query()`): the awards a game won whose name says game of the year or best game, the newest first, 6 at most, with their year when Wikidata gives it. The query chooses the awards by name before joining anything, which is what makes it light enough (the earlier try over every award was too heavy); it was written without access to the service, so the first `build` shows whether it answers.
- **About** (`wd.about`, from `tools/extracts.py`): the first few hundred characters of the game's Wikipedia page (Wikipedia's summary service), in the visitor's language when it is English or French, else English; Wikipedia's text, CC BY-SA 4.0: the sheet says so and links the page. It is the only description on a sheet: the stores' are not kept.
- **Entry points** (`explore.js`): each genre, theme, mode, perspective, style, mood, gameplay, content, access, series, developer and publisher chip on a sheet links to the sheet's platform browse view filtered by it (`#<platform>/browse?developers=<value>`): that is a studio's, a series' or a genre's page. A value too rare for the platform's facets (`FACET_MIN`) shows no game there. A search across all platforms at once is not done: each platform has its own list of names.
- **Search over every platform** (`explore.js`): the "All platforms" button next to the search box (`#all/search/<words>`) asks every platform's names list (`x/<platform>/names.json`, about 5 MB for the 45, only when asked) and lists the best known matches first, 200 at most, each with its platform. A game on several platforms appears once for each.
- **Saved views** (`explore.js`): "Save this view" names the browse view's filters and sort and keeps them in this browser (`localStorage` key `atlas-views`, 50 at most, a link each with a remove button); nothing is sent anywhere, and without storage the button keeps nothing.
- The look is Boréale's (`branding/README.md` in JigSawFr/boreale), and so are the icons, the favicons and the preview image in `assets/` (`branding/atlas/`, copied by its `tools/sync_branding.py`: change them there): the night, the aurora once at the top, Sora, Figtree and JetBrains Mono served with the site. What Boréale's sites share is [boreale-site](https://github.com/JigSawFr/boreale-site), the submodule `library/boreale-site`: the fonts, the marks (`marks/atlas.svg` is Atlas's: the frame in the aurora's colours, a folded map and the pole star in snow), `boreale.css` and `boreale.js` (the colours, the language menu and the menu of Boréale's sites), `chrome.css` and `chrome.js` (the page chrome: sky and aurora, header, sections, buttons, cards, band, footer), `lang.js`, `umami.js` and the texts of the two menus, copied into `assets/` at the build (`boreale.copy_assets()`); `social-preview.html` is the source of `assets/social-preview.png`, taken with Chromium at 1280 × 640.
- **The header**, as PlayGuard's: the home page's sections and the status as plain links, **Explore** lit by the aurora to the explorer, GitHub, then the languages; on a phone the links on a second row to scroll, Explore first. The header, the footer and the sky come from boreale-site (`Site.header()`, `Site.footer()`, `Site.sky()`, written by `build_site.py`). The home page has the aurora drawn by `chrome.js` behind its header and hero, the other pages a quieter sky. The name opens the menu of Boréale's sites (boreale-site's `Site.switcher()` and `sites.json`), a popover placed by its `boreale.js`. A link to another of them keeps the origin and carries `utm_source=atlas&utm_medium=nav` (`Site.links_out()`, over every page). Unlike Lantern's, it shows no versions: the build reads nothing from boreale.dev.
- The Content-Security-Policy allows nothing but the site itself: no inline script (the explorer's texts are `application/json` data), no style attribute, no font or script from elsewhere; the one address a page may send to is Umami's (`connect-src`).
- **The audience**, counted by Umami Cloud (<https://cloud.umami.is>): `assets/umami.js` (boreale-site's copy) is its tracker, served with the site, that sends each page seen to `gateway.umami.is`, without cookie, and only from the site's own address (`SITE_URL`'s, not a local copy). Its site in Umami (boreale-site's `UMAMI_WEBSITE`) is Boréale's, shared with the other projects' sites: Umami's *Hostname* filter tells them apart. The footer links to boreale.dev's privacy and legal notice, which says what it counts.
- `.github/workflows/site.yml` builds it and publishes it to GitHub Pages after each successful `build` or `daily` run, at each push to main touching it, and on demand.
